## 1 Nov 2026 — Stage 2

- **`SourceRef` kind `bill`** (with `bill_id` and `field_path`): a value billx read from the client's own bill is neither a regulation nor a client statement. It links to the stored bill PDF at the value's page (`/bills/{id}/file#page=N`), and `SourcedValue.is_sourced` counts it as sourced. The JSON Schema `source_ref.schema.json` gains the kind with the two fields required.
- **A `needs_review` bill becomes usable when every value intake takes from it is verified by billx or confirmed by the client** (one click, or a correction). Required: bill month, energy (kWh or kVAh), recorded MD, contract demand, tariff category, and each ToD slot's energy when printed. The bill row then reads `accepted_by: bill_confirmations`. This is the per-field confirmation of invariant 7.
- **The bill PDF is stored** (`data/bill_files/<sha256>.pdf`, gitignored) so a bill citation opens the page; ergPLAN never reads it (billx does). The same file is never sent to billx twice.
- **Industry shapes are parametric YAML** (base level, working blocks per weekday, dips, ramps) that expand to the 672-value week the master prompt describes, rather than 672 literal values: easier to read, review and edit; `profile_synthesis.shape()` produces the 7 × 96 array.
- **Synthesis algorithm**: (1) the shape laid over the month, weekly offs and shutdowns at base; (2) each ToD window scaled to its energy (exact); (3) deviations from each window's mean stretched by one factor (bisection) until the month's maximum equals MD × PF — window energies unchanged. When that stretch would need negative load (flat-topped shapes), the stretch stops while the lowest block stays above half the shape's minimum and the rest of the MD is placed as one 15-minute block at the busiest block, its energy taken pro rata from the same window. Stored kW is rounded to the watt and each month's rounding residue is put back one watt at a time on blocks near the month's median, so stored monthly energy equals the bill exactly. Months without a bill: mean daily energy of the billed months, flagged extrapolated.
- **kVAh-only months** are converted to kWh with the month's power factor (0.95 assumed when absent) for the profile; the stored intake keeps the bill's unit.
- **A ToD split whose slot names carry no hours is not used** (the month's total is), with a warning to the client, rather than guessing which order window `Tod-1` means.
- **IEX vintages**: one vintage per file; a file overlapping a live vintage of its segment is refused (409) unless `replace`, which marks the old vintages superseded and keeps their rows (pinned runs replay identically). Duplicate (day, block) rows: identical ones dropped; disagreeing ones resolved to the latest `Scraped At` (or the later row) and counted. The jouleWise files are `.xlsx` with sheet `15min`; CSV is accepted too.
- **Real-bill e2e runs on its own database** (`ergplan_e2e`) so it can run while the unit suite truncates `ergplan_test`.

## 1 Nov 2026 — Stage 3

- **Rules are loaded in the API layer and passed to pure engines.** `engine/` takes `RuleView`s and quantities; `platform/routers/cost.py` reads `rules_approved` through `rulebook.repository` (invariant 2) and calls the engines. `engine/scope.py` builds the context and picks rows; the scope operators stay in `rulebook/scope.py` (tested there), so one grammar serves the analyst sandbox and the engines.
- **Excess demand is charged in addition to the demand charge on the whole recorded demand**, at the row's multiple of the demand rate on the excess: UPERC general provisions 7(ii)(b) ("200% of the normal rate apart from the normal fixed / demand charges") and MERC's 150% clause read the same way.
- **A bill is evaluated on the tariff in force at its period end** (`period.to`, else the bill month). Avoided cost uses the tariff in force on `as_of` (default today) with each profile month choosing its seasonal and ToD rows: the plan faces today's tariff, not last year's.
- **Replay groups lines** (demand, excess demand, energy and ToD together, wheeling, FPPCA, duty, PF, LF, prompt payment, minimum, taxes, and each unmodelled line by its label), because DISCOMs print ToD inside energy (NPCL, UPPCL) or as a separate line (MSEDCL). Credits (rebates, subsidies) are negative. Printed totals and payable amounts, arrears and adjustments are left out. When a layout prints energy only as ToD zone information lines, those are the billed energy.
- **ToD zones on the bill are matched to the approved slots by the adjustment the bill applied** (a zone printed at its full rate, base × (1 + p), or as the adjustment alone, base × p). A zone whose adjustment no approved slot carries is put on the nearest slot and reported, so a wrong rule shows as an unexplained energy difference. This is how the MSEDCL peak rule (+20% read, +25% billed) was found and corrected from the MYT order's footnote.
- **Bill status is the worst of its line statuses.** A line is explained only by a named gap (engine/gaps.py, listed in known-gaps) or a billx `printed_inconsistency` flag. `demand_proration` is recognised only when billed / computed equals days / (365/12) within 0.02%.
- **Bills dated before the approved tariffs are replayed as `tariff_period`** (explained, nothing computed) rather than against a later order.
- **The marginal avoided cost is a 12 × 96 matrix**, because the retail rate depends only on month and time of day; the 35,040 block values are that matrix laid over the calendar. Components the rulebook does not hold (FPPCA, duty) are left out and named.
- **kVAh-billed schedules convert to INR per kWh with the bills' kVAh/kWh ratio** (bill-sourced), else 1 / the bills' power factor, else 1 / 0.95 assumed.
- **`SourceRef.url` is a computed field** in every serialised ref (`/documents/{id}#page=N` or `/bills/{id}/file#page=N`) and is dropped on input, so refs round-trip.
- **Rulebook corrections found by the replay were approved by `build-agent@ergplan`** through the normal gate (duplicate, edit with review note and field citation, golden tests, approve): MSEDCL `tod_slots` v3 and NPCL and UPPCL `tariff_schedule` v2. The golden test "peak +20%" was wrong and was corrected.
- **The replay test runs on billx's stored readings of the real bills** (`data/bills/billx-results/<sha256>.json`, gitignored), so it needs no network; reading a bill through billx again would cost about USD 0.2 each.

## 1 Nov 2026 — Stage 4

- **Landed cost is computed per ToD slot of a chosen month** (default the current month) with the tariff and OA rules in force on `as_of` (default today). Open-access sources do not vary by slot (their price and charges are flat); IEX varies by slot through the vintage's mean clearing price over that slot's blocks in that calendar month; the grid is the avoided cost of the slot. The 15-minute series is `total = a × P + b` (`Waterfall.coefficient()`), which Stage 6 applies block by block.
- **Loss gross-up applies to the price and the per-kWh charges levied on injection** (STU transmission, wheeling, ISTS per kWh): (P + charges) × (1 / Π(1 − loss) − 1), over the loss stages whose rows apply (ISTS loss only for inter-state; STU loss; distribution loss by voltage).
- **Exemptions are lines**, negative, citing the exemption's `waived_pct` field: `captive_26_51`, `group_captive`, `geoa_contract_demand_not_reduced`, `behind_meter`, `re_source_waiver`, `consumer_voltage_at_or_above_kv`, `commissioned_in_band`; a sunset date before `as_of` switches it off; `other` is shown with the row and never applied automatically.
- **The banking charge is shown on the full injected energy and labelled as the upper bound** (master prompt Stage 4); the settlement simulator (Stage 5) decides the banked quantity.
- **Default PPA prices are assumptions** (editable chips) until the client states a quote; LCOE (rooftop) and LCOS (battery) use normative assumptions (capex, O&M, CUF, degradation, life, 10% cost of capital). The behind-the-meter battery is charged from the cheapest grid ToD slot ÷ round-trip efficiency. Rooftop energy is offered only in slots overlapping 07:00–17:00.
- **IEX prices cite their vintage** with a new `market` source-ref kind (`vintage_id`, resolving to `/prices/vintages/{id}`, which names the file and its SHA-256) — invariant 8.
- **OA rule scopes corrected**: the UP ISTS loss (1.56%) applied to intra-state transactions in its approved scope; NPCL and UPPCL wheeling, distribution loss, CSS and additional surcharge were scoped to intra-state only although the orders apply them to all OA consumers using the distribution network. New versions approved through the gate with review notes.
- **The six-consumer spreadsheets are drafted by the build agent** from the approved pages, computed by hand independently of the engine, and await jouleWise's approval (the master prompt asks the person to supply or approve them). Writing them caught one mis-read band in my own draft (132 kV is "above 66 up to 132 kV": CSS 0.33, not 0.14).
- **CUF profiles (packages/profiles/cuf) are not created in Stage 4**: no capacity-based OA charge is approved in UP or Maharashtra yet, so scheduled energy enters only through the scenario's capacity and CUF; Stage 5 adds the curves it needs for settlement.

## 1 Nov 2026 — Stage 5

- **One settlement rule governs a consumer's bank**: the rule matching the first source; other sources whose scope it covers bank into it, the rest are netted without banking (their surplus is reported as sold or curtailed). Separate banks per scope come with the optimizer if a client mixes intra-state captive and ISTS third-party power under different rules.
- **Aggregated netting (tod_slot_monthly, monthly, annual) is spread back to blocks**: consumption-side quantities (set-off credit, withdrawals, grid) by each block's load, supply-side quantities (set-off at source, deposits, charge, unbanked) by each block's injection. Both identities hold in every block; set-off is reported both ways (`setoff_kwh`, `setoff_at_source_kwh`).
- **In aggregated netting, all of a month's deposits are made before any withdrawal** (the month is one settlement); in block and hourly netting the bank works in time order.
- **The banking charge in kind is taken on deposit**; an INR/kWh charge does not reduce energy (it is a cost line for the optimizer).
- **Carry-over ceiling** (UP CRE Reg. 12) is applied at each banking period's end from that period's injection and DISCOM drawal; FIFO lots; everything left lapses at the financial year's end.
- **Slot classes**: blocks carry the DISCOM's slot name and the class a rule may use (`peak` = a slot with a ToD surcharge, `off_peak` otherwise), per the Stage 1 binding decision.
- **Normative generation curves** (`packages/profiles/cuf`, UP and MH solar and wind) are assumptions scaled to an annual CUF, used until a developer's profile is supplied; the settle endpoint says so in its notes.
- **Real OA settlement statements were not supplied** (`data/settlements/` is empty), so the "reproduce a year within 0.5%" check cannot run; recorded as a known gap.

## 1 Nov 2026 — Stage 6

- **Three passes instead of one 35,040-block MILP.** The full-year 15-minute model with a battery and the bank did not finish inside two minutes on this two-core workspace (no solution after 77 s). The solve is now: (1) **sizing** on hourly blocks (the 15-minute year summed to 8,760 hours, rates energy-weighted); (2) **dispatch** at 15 minutes, month by month, with the sizes fixed, carrying the battery's charge and the bank forward (each month must leave the battery as charged as it found it); (3) **settlement pass** at 15 minutes per banking period. The 5 MVA test sites solve in about 30 s (MSEDCL) and 75 s (PVVNL). `resolution_min: 60` runs the whole thing hourly (preview).
- **The solver settles its own dispatch under the rule's order, then the judge settles it again.** A plain LP lets the bank do what the regulation does not: it banked energy that the rule sets off directly (UP's 07:00–16:00 rebate makes a banked kWh worth 0.92 of a normal-hour kWh, more than a set-off kWh in the rebate slot) and timed withdrawals to the dearest blocks; the judge differed by 1.5 GWh. So: in sizing, a **deposit guard** charges each banked kWh what banking could earn over setting it off in its own block (best withdrawal rate in the period × (1 − charge in kind) − own rate, floored at zero, plus a paisa) — the solver never gains by banking what the rule sets off, at the price of under-valuing genuine surplus by at most that spread (a conservative bias); in the **settlement pass** the dispatch is fixed (read to the watt-hour, exactly as the judge reads it) and the objective is lexicographic: direct set-off first, then deposits over unbanked surplus, then withdrawals as early as the windows allow in the rule's bucket priority, then the least lapse at each period end (earlier lapse weighs more). Both test sites agree with the judge to 0 kWh.
- **Carry-over ceiling in sizing takes the lower of two ceilings** (linear, conservative); the settlement pass applies "the higher of" exactly with one binary per period. A binary per period in the sizing MILP with a big-M sized to the whole period's supply left a 2.5–2.8 % MIP gap at the time limit.
- **The comparison is month by month** on grid energy, set-off, banked, charge, withdrawn and lapsed, plus the year's totals, against `ergplan_resettle_tolerance_kwh` (1 kWh). Costs shown to anyone come from the judge's quantities; the solver's objective is shown to analysts beside the re-settled cost.
- **Per-kWh landed cost comes from Stage 4's `landed_cost.evaluate()` per month and ToD slot**, without the banking upper bound (the model and the judge settle banking). Capacity charges (INR/MW) enter per kWh at the source's normative CUF, which equals the per-MW charge exactly for the normative curve. IEX purchases are dispatchable up to the stated cap at the vintage's mean clearing price per month and slot (the same `market` ref as Stage 4); block-level IEX prices are a later refinement.
- **Owned plant costs** (rooftop PV, behind-the-meter battery) are CRF × capex + O&M from Stage 4's normative assumptions (`lcoe.default_rooftop`, `default_bess`); first-year energy (no degradation in the dispatch); battery round-trip 88 % split evenly between charge and discharge, 1–4 hours. Default upper bounds when the analyst gives none: rooftop = roof area ÷ 10 m² per kWp, else the contract demand; battery = half the contract demand; OA = three times the contract demand. Each is named in the binding list when it binds.
- **Payback and IRR** use the capital of owned plant only (open-access PPAs have none): yearly cash saving = saving + the annualised capital already inside the "after" cost; IRR over the shortest economic life among the owned assets.
- **Emissions** use a grid factor of 0.71 tCO2/MWh as an assumption (CEA CO2 Baseline Database, recent versions 0.71–0.73) unless the scenario states one; the chip says which version to report against.
- **Runs live in `platform/runs.py`**, not `optimizer/scenarios.py`: the layout keeps `optimizer/` pure, so database reads, pins and storage sit with the API; `optimizer/scenarios.py` holds the spec and the money arithmetic.
- **Replay reads the pinned versions through `repository.pinned_rules`** (approved or since superseded, never a draft, effective on the date) — the one read path besides `rules_approved`, used only when `runs.replay_of` is set. The pins record every version the run read (a superset of those that bound a number).
- **15-minute site solves are marked `slow`** and excluded from the watcher (each takes one to two minutes); CI runs them in their own step and the Stage 6 end-to-end runs them. The hourly integration tests run in the watcher.
- **Sensitivities hold the sizes** and reprice: a source's price ±x % scales that source's energy line (price and its charges); a grid-rate ±x % scales today's cost and the plan's grid energy line. Re-solving per sensitivity is a later option.
- **Floats stop at the solver.** HiGHS works in binary floats; the problem carries its money and energy inputs twice — floats for the solver, Decimals (`optimizer.model.Exact`, built from the same Decimal rule values and profile) for every reported cost. Solver outputs (capacities, dispatch) become Decimals in one place (`optimizer.resettle.dec`, to the watt-hour) before the judge settles them; nothing stored or returned is a float. Synthetic test problems without `Exact` read their float inputs back through their shortest decimal form.
